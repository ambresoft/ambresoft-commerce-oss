# Product sales

Authorized follow-up: a product editor **Sale** tab, retaining the native dashboard UI. This is our implementation of the requested behavior, without copying upstream code or schema. [OpenCart discount documentation](https://docs.opencart.com/en-gb/catalog/product/discount) and the [3.0.3.8 cart implementation](https://github.com/opencart/opencart/blob/3.0.3.8/upload/system/library/cart/cart.php) establish the reference quantity / priority / price selection; the optional sale-stock quota and percentage mode are additional requirements.

## Rules and ownership

Pricing owns `ProductSaleRules` (framework-free decimal arithmetic and selection) and the `ProductSales` persistence adapter. Catalog's existing product save, permissions, optimistic version and transaction own editing and duplication. Sale configuration is shared across languages; customer-group names use their existing locale-linked content. At most 100 editable rows per product.

Each row optionally targets one customer group (blank means all customers), minimum purchase quantity (default 1), priority (default 1, lower wins), fixed final unit price or percentage off the regular product price, optional stock quota, and optional start/end calendar dates. The highest qualifying minimum quantity wins, then the lowest priority, lowest resulting rounded unit price, and finally stable ID. Rules do not stack and never increase the regular price. Percentages range from 0 through 100; amounts use decimal strings / bcmath and half-away-from-zero currency rounding. Fixed prices follow primary-currency precision and conversion; percentages do not change during currency conversion. Existing orders keep their own saved prices and currency.

Rules with no selected group apply to every customer, including guests and installations without a default group. For group-specific rules, guests and customers without memberships use the configured default group. Otherwise any active group the customer belongs to can qualify; inactive groups do not produce new sale prices. New assignments require active groups; an existing inactive assignment can remain in the editor. Groups referenced by sale rules cannot be deleted.

Blank dates mean no bound. Both date boundaries include the whole calendar day in Settings → General's store timezone. Blank sale stock means no separate quota: all current/future product stock qualifies, subject to the ordinary product stock rules. A numeric quota is a lifetime allocation for that sale row and does not create inventory. Zero leaves no discounted units available. A rule must cover the whole requested quantity; there is no mixed regular/sale pricing within one line. If no row qualifies, normal product pricing applies.

## Orders and stock limits

Order quotes resolve eligible new lines before tax calculation. An explicit staff unit-price override takes precedence. Quotes do not reserve anything. Order creation reserves the full sale quantity in its transaction even when the initial status does not deduct physical stock, preventing concurrent orders from promising the same limited discount. Existing lines retain their price snapshot on edits; increasing their quantity must fit the saved sale's remaining quota. A changed explicit unit-price override clears that line's sale allocation. Removing a line releases its allocation; failed saves roll back pricing, reservations, items, totals and stock together.

A status whose stock action is `restore` releases sale allocations while preserving their association for history; moving to a non-restore status re-reserves the saved quantity and refuses the transition if the quota cannot cover it. Physical stock still follows the existing independent stock-action rules. Every affected product row is locked in ID order before price selection / quota writes / stock movement. Allocation writes also lock their sale row. PostgreSQL guards enforce product ownership and prevent over-allocation or reducing a quota below allocated units, including writes bypassing the adapter.

Removing an unused rule deletes it; removing a rule referenced by an order archives it (`is_enabled=false`), stops new discounted orders and preserves saved prices / quota history. Editing amounts, groups, thresholds or schedules affects new orders; already allocated units still count toward the row's quota. Duplication copies enabled rules with fresh IDs and no allocation history; the copied product still starts with zero stock. Permanently deleting a product removes its sale configuration and allocations while existing order line price snapshots survive.

## Persistence and delivery

`product_sales` belongs to products (cascade) and optional customer groups (restrict), with checks for nonnegative fixed amounts, percentages 0–100, quantities/priorities, optional nonnegative quotas, and ordered dates. `product_sale_allocations` has one row per order item and cascades with the item or its product sale. Both tables participate in the central audit ledger. Product edits remain guarded by `products.update`; order operations keep their existing capabilities. No separate sale menu or capability is introduced.

Migration `2026_10_16_000300_create_product_sales.php` is additive: existing products get no rules and retain their prices. Apply with `docker compose exec -T app php artisan migrate`. Rollback discards sale rules and reservation history, restores ordinary pricing for future new orders, and preserves order amounts already saved. Migration `2026_10_16_000400_make_product_sale_groups_optional.php` makes the group nullable without changing existing assignments. Rolling that migration back requires assigning a group to all global rules first; rollback refuses to silently narrow their audience. Storefront rendering remains the later storefront phase; this feature is operational in the existing admin order workflow and its pricing resolver is reusable there.

Verification covers row persistence/validation/permissions/versions/copying; amount/quantity/priority/date selection; customer groups; fixed-price currency conversion; order quota reservations, overrides, cancellation/reactivation, archived rules and product removal; and two real PostgreSQL connections racing for the last discounted unit. Native React checks cover adding/removing percentage rules and preserving blank stock and entered values across tabs.
